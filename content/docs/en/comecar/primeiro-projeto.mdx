# Your first project

Let's put together a small GDD (about a dozen pages) so you get a feel for the flow. The example is a **fantasy RPG**, but the script works for any genre.

<Callout type="info" title="No rush">
  You don't have to create everything in one go. The app saves on every change
  and you can come back to any step whenever you want.
</Callout>

## Before you start

Prerequisites:

- A GDD Manager account (free signup at <a href="/login">/login</a>)
- 15 minutes
- The urge to invent cool names (you'll need it)

## Step 1 — Create the project

On the home page (`/`), click **"+ New project"**.

Give it a name (e.g. `Tutorial Adventure`) and a short description. The project starts with the default **five-group** structure:

| Group | What goes inside |
|---|---|
| 📖 Overview | Pitch, target audience, what makes the game different |
| 🎮 Game Design | Core loop, central mechanics, progression |
| 📦 Game Content | Characters, items, enemies, world, narrative |
| 🎨 Presentation | Art, audio, UX/UI, controls |
| 🏭 Production | Technology, roadmap, risks, monetization |

Those five are the skeleton. Everything you write from here finds its place inside one of them.

## Step 2 — Write the Overview

Start with the page that gives every other page its context.

1. Click **📖 Overview** in the sidebar.
2. Click the description area and start writing.

The editor works like a modern document: type `/` to open the block menu (heading, list, table, image, callout). Write the pitch in two or three sentences, who it's for, and what makes the game different.

<Callout type="tip" title="Use callouts to change register">
  Type <code>/callout</code> and pick a variant. <strong>Note</strong> for
  context, <strong>Warning</strong> for a gotcha, <strong>Design decision</strong>
  to record why you chose one path over another. Six months later, the "why" is
  what you'll be most glad you wrote down.
</Callout>

## Step 3 — Lay out the tree before writing everything

Resist the urge to finish every page now. First create the **titles** of the pages you know will exist.

1. Hover a group in the sidebar and click the `+`.
2. Type the name and hit Enter.
3. Repeat.

For `📦 Game Content`, say: `Characters`, `Enemies`, `Items`, `Economy`, `World`. Inside `Characters`, create `Warrior` and `Mage`.

In five minutes you have the map of the document. Writing gets far easier once you already know where everything lives.

<Callout type="tip" title="Shortcut">
  Press <code>N</code> anywhere in the project to open the quick new-page modal —
  you can pick the name and where it goes without leaving the keyboard.
</Callout>

## Step 4 — Connect the pages

This is where the GDD stops being a pile of files.

Inside any page's text, type `$[` and the name of another page. The app autocompletes it and turns it into a clickable link.

Example, on the `Warrior` page:

> The Warrior leans on melee damage and sustain. He's the direct counterpoint to the $[Mage], who trades resilience for range. His gear comes out of the $[Economy] priced above average.

Now, when someone reads the Warrior page, they reach the Mage in one click — and the Mage knows it's referenced.

## Step 5 — Give a DataID to the pages that matter

On pages that stand for concrete things in the game (an item, an enemy, a currency), it's worth setting a stable nickname.

1. **Right below the title** there's a discreet field: click **"+ DataID"**.
2. The app suggests one based on the title — e.g. `DATA_WARRIOR`.
3. Accept or edit it (worth keeping a convention across the project).

You don't need this on lore or process pages. It's for the ones you reference a lot and the ones the AI will need to find later.

## Step 6 — Look at the document from another angle

With the tree laid out and a few pages written, it's worth stepping back:

- **Mind map** (`/mindmap`) — the tree becomes a graph. Empty branches jump out, and you can see which parts of the game are still just a title.
- **Document mode** (`/view`) — the whole GDD as one continuous read, in tree order. That's the view you send to someone outside the team.
- **Export** (`/export`) — the same document as Markdown, PDF or Word.
